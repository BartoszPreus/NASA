Written with SI assistance (Claude Code / Antigravity). First pass 2026-11-15 (commit af51fbd); corrected 2026-11-15 after the review on issue #2; second pass J35 (issue #21) 2026-11-15: analog catalogues, LPI analog workshop volumes, OpenAlex, LPSC/EGU abstracts and Russian- and Chinese-language searches, sources `docs/sources/j35.md`. Data: `data/sites/novelty_check.csv`; pipeline outputs that read it: `data/sites/ranking.csv`, `data/sites/sites.geojson` (column `novelty`); sources: `docs/sources/j22.md`.

Until a person has done the check of section 5, every verdict below is what an SI model found, not a reviewed finding.

## 1. Why

The challenge asks not to rediscover well-known analogs. A site is only called a candidate after someone has looked for it in the analog literature. This page records that search for the final ranking (`data/sites/ranking.csv`, target `base_regions`, the seven lunar south-pole candidate base regions of `data/moon/reference.json`).

## 2. Method

1. **Sites.** The 15 highest-ranked sites with `is_known = False` and `looks_like_artefact` not `yes` are ranks 1 to 16 without rank 8. They are four regions, so the literature question was asked once per region and then carried to each site. The list was extended to the whole top 30 plus ranks 33 and 34, which covers seven regions: Putorana Plateau, southern Peary Land, Hazen Plateau (northern Ellesmere Island), northern Baffin Island, northern Ungava Peninsula, Polar Urals, Chukotka Peninsula; plus Novaya Zemlya at rank 33.
2. **Identification.** Nearest named feature, country, access and protection, from OpenStreetMap (Nominatim, Overpass), Wikipedia and the protected-area pages listed in `docs/sources/j22.md`. These pages identify the places; none of them is evidence of analog use (see 3).
3. **Literature search.** Per region: "<region> lunar analog", "<region> Mars analog", "<region> planetary analogue", and the regional name with impact, permafrost or polar desert. A hit counts only if its page was opened and the analog statement is quoted in `novelty_check.csv`; a search snippet is UNVERIFIED. The review of 15 Nov read the 24 Wikipedia pages of the first pass and found no analog statement in any of them; the six "snippet" sources of the first pass were opened on 15 Nov and resolve to unrelated articles (Andean violence, sparkling wine yeast, Arctic thaw slumps without the Hazen Plateau, vaccine cost; one failed to load). Those sources are dropped.
   **Second pass (J35, issue #21).** Per region: the ESA Catalogue of Planetary Analogues (Preston, Grady and Barber 2012, 171 pages, 30 site chapters, full text searched), NASA's Planetary Analog Explorer and Planetary Analogs pages, the NASA Ames Arctic analog page, the IMEWG "Mars on Earth" page, the Europlanet TA1 field-site list, the two LPI "Analog sites for Mars missions" volumes (LPI Contributions 1612 and 1738, full text searched), an OpenAlex title-and-abstract search "<region> AND (analog OR analogue) AND (Mars OR Martian OR Moon OR lunar OR planetary)", web searches restricted to the LPSC/LPI abstract servers, and searches in Russian (Putorana, Polar Urals, Chukotka, Novaya Zemlya) and Chinese (Ngari, Chang Tang, Aksai Chin). None of the regions is listed in any catalogue. Every catalogue and search, with or without a hit, is in `docs/sources/j35.md`. Google Scholar answered the automated request with a redirect (HTTP 302 to google.com/sgsync), which was not followed, and the NASA ADS search API needs a token; neither was searched directly (ADS records were reached through OpenAlex and the LPI servers). The Russian searches found nothing; a Russian reader has not confirmed them (issue #11).
4. **Verdict.** `established`: inside a region of `data/reference/known_analogs.csv`. `mentioned`: an opened page names the region (not the site) as an analog. **One rule for the unit (review of 15 Nov, issue #21):** the "region" is the named land unit that contains the site, from a plateau or peninsula up to the whole island or peninsula it lies on (Peary Land, Ungava Peninsula, Baffin Island, Ellesmere Island). A page counts if it names that unit, or a named place inside it, as an analog; it does not count if it names only a neighbouring unit or a whole country or "the Arctic". The rule leans towards `mentioned`, because a novelty claim should err on the side of "already in the literature". Under it northern Baffin Island ("the high Arctic regions around Baffin and Victoria Islands", Up Here 2019) and the Hazen Plateau ("Ellesmere Island as an analog of Kasei Valles, Mars", Arfstrom, LPSC 2015, title) are both `mentioned`, both on island-level statements of the same strength; neither page names the site's plateau or upland. `unreported`: "not found in the analog catalogues and searches listed here".
5. **What the numbers say.** For every candidate the only claim the data support is: slope and roughness statistics at 240 m to 1.6 km fall inside the range of the lunar south-pole candidate regions. Nothing in this repository compares a site with lunar mare terrain or measures mechanical or chemical properties. Two facts go with every Arctic row: the land-cover class "moss and lichen" counts as bare, so the plateau tops are mostly tundra: with ESA WorldCover class 60 "bare / sparse vegetation" only, none of the 16 Putorana sites and only 3 of the 30 rows have a site of the strict ranking within 3 km, and the strict top 30 has no Putorana, Peary Land, Ungava, Baffin, Chukotka or Polar Urals site (`data/validation/landcover_sensitivity.json`, `full_run.overlap.top30`, `full_run.regions_top30`; `data/sites/top30_review.csv`); and the coldest monthly night mean of these sites is 235 to 250 K (the 28 Arctic sites of the top 30, `nasa_modis_lst_night_min_k`) against 51 to 141 K in the lunar regions (`data/sites/partner_context.csv`, `docs/sources/j24.md`).
6. **Validation is not evidence of novelty.** Around known lunar analog sites the share of lunar-like ground is higher than around random places, a borderline result (1.02 against 0.39; 2.8% of random runs reach it), and at the scale of one-degree tiles the score does not separate known analog tiles from the rest (`docs/VALIDATION.md`, issue #1). This report therefore does not argue "the method recovers known analogs, so its other top sites are probably analogs".

## 4. Conclusions

1. **Nothing may be called a discovery.** Of the 28 sites of the top 30 outside every known analog radius (`is_known = False`), 18 are "not found in the analog catalogues and searches listed here" (16 Putorana Plateau, Chukotka, Polar Urals) and 10 lie in regions that an opened page names as Mars analogs (southern Peary Land 5, Hazen Plateau 1, northern Ungava 2, northern Baffin Island 2). Southern Peary Land, rank 1 of the ranking, is named as a Mars analogue in a 2025 PhD thesis and in EGU and LPI abstracts: it must not be presented as absent from the literature.
2. **The top of the ranking is seven Arctic regions and two known analog regions.** Putorana Plateau basalt tablelands (16 sites) and southern Peary Land polar desert (5 sites) dominate. The Putorana Plateau is the largest group the second pass did not find in any catalogue or search (English, Russian), a negative of limited strength: Google Scholar and NASA ADS were not searched directly and no Russian reader has checked the Russian searches (issue #11); its ground is mostly moss-and-lichen tundra (section 2, item 5). Their slope and roughness statistics at 240 m to 1.6 km fall inside the range of the lunar south-pole candidate regions; their ground is partly tundra, and their coldest monthly night mean (235 K or more) is at least 94 K above the warmest lunar region (141 K).
3. **Four sites have not been seen.** Ranks 14 and 30 (hazy) and 22 and 29 (cloudy) have no clear satellite image; their closed-depression share is 0.0, so no lakes or hollows are claimed for them.

## 6. Wording rule

Use "not found in the analog catalogues and searches listed here", never "unknown" or "discovered". Anything not opened is UNVERIFIED.
