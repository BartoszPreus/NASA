# Judge questions and answers

1. **What would a mission team do with this?** Start with the regional shortlist, open the imagery and evidence for each location, and pick where to investigate mobility, slope or landing terrain further. A field decision would also need access, geology and local safety checks.
2. **Why focus on terrain shape?** Slope, roughness and depressions can be computed consistently from elevation models on both bodies. Weather, regolith, radiation and illumination matter to field suitability and need separate evidence.
3. **How is NASA data central?** NASA LOLA provides the lunar elevation reference and a published check of lunar slopes. Independent ICESat-2 tracks check Earth grid slopes. NASA Earth imagery, meteorological data and LRO instrument products give context for interpreting matches.
4. **Does the slope test validate the whole ranking?** The NASA slope-map test measures agreement for a dedicated plane-fit estimator. The ranking uses two-point slopes along with roughness and depressions. Each check has its own scope.
5. **Why compare laser profiles and grids?** Laser tracks sample along directions and across footprints that differ from the grid. ICESat-2 gives an independent Earth comparison, with laser-to-grid slope ratios of 0.43–1.16 at 240 m for the selected sites.
6. **What happens when you change the Earth elevation data?** Copernicus and NASADEM produced similar slopes while depression and candidate maps differed. On their common coverage, Spearman was 0.637 and 21 of the top 50 tiles were shared. Copernicus remains the primary input under the stated rule.
7. **What did the known analog test tell you?** At the site scale, the median enrichment was 0.91× the scan-wide bare-cell rate, versus 0.36× for matched random circles. At the one-degree tile scale, the corrected recovery AUC was 0.550 with a 90% interval of 0.418–0.682, consistent with chance.
8. **Why do sand seas appear?** Dune hollows can register as depressions and their slopes can pass the similarity test. A relief-to-roughness flag puts likely dune fields last. It can also flag real analog terrain, so the flags stay visible.
9. **Are any of the places discoveries?** J22 found established analogs, sites mentioned in the literature, and a place it did not find in the catalogues and searches it lists. That last verdict describes the search evidence. A person should open the evidence identified in J22 before featuring a candidate site.
10. **What about Mars?** The current reference covers the lunar south-pole regions. Applying this method to Mars would require choosing suitable Mars elevation data, recalibrating feature scales and validating the result against Mars-specific terrain and known Earth analogs.
