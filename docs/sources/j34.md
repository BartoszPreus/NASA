# J34: Sentinel-1 C-band backscatter per site

Issue #20. Code: [`pipeline/radar_context.py`](../../pipeline/radar_context.py). Outputs: [`data/sites/radar_context.csv`](../../data/sites/radar_context.csv), [`data/sites/radar_context_summary.json`](../../data/sites/radar_context_summary.json). Stage: `python -m pipeline.run radar-context`. Written with SI assistance (Claude Code).

**Read this first.** Sentinel-1 is C band with linear polarisation (VV+VH, or HH+HV over the polar regions). It is not the circular polarisation ratio (CPR) of LRO Mini-RF, and these values cannot be compared with Mini-RF CPR. Cross-pol backscatter responds to volume scattering (vegetation, rough or blocky rock). It is context and does not test the ranking. Nothing here enters the terrain score.

Quoted: "The use of these data fall under the terms and conditions of the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International Public License. Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data." It also says "Global coverage comprises all land masses and ice sheets from 82 degrees northern to 79 degrees southern latitude" and gives the period "from 1-Dec-2019 to 30-Nov-2020".
- Documentation page: http://sentinel-1-global-coherence-earthbigdata.s3-website-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/. Quoted: "Backscatter amplitudes ( AMP.tif files): γ0 [dB] = 20*log10(DN) - 83, γ0 [power] = DN^2 / 199526231, No data value 0". It also says "Mean backscatter (γ0) for VV, VH, HH, and HV polarizations", with seasons "northern hemisphere winter (DJF), spring (MAM), summer (JJA), and fall (SON)". The layover/shadow codes are "1 TESTED Neither layover nor shadow, 5 LAYOVER ..., 16 SHADOW ..., 21 LAYOVER IN SHADOW". The tile name is "referenced to upper left tile corner". The same page quotes the same CC BY 4.0 licence and "Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data."

## Method

- Sites: the default top 30 (`data/sites/ranking.csv` with polygons from `sites.geojson`) and the strict-mask top 30 (`data/validation/landcover_sensitivity/ranking_bare_strict.csv` with polygons from `sites_bare_strict.geojson`). Also the 28 `known` sites of `data/sites/partner_context.csv`, with polygons from `sites.geojson`. The CSV has one row per site and group, 88 rows in all. A site in two groups appears twice.
- Season: the summer of the site's hemisphere, JJA 2020 north of the equator and DJF 2019-20 south of it. These are seasonal means of many scenes, not one scene. For that reason a single date, orbit direction and scene incidence angle do not apply. The CSV gives the median local incidence angle over all relative orbits of the tile (`local_incidence_median_deg`) and the number of orbits (`n_orbits`).
- Per site: 3 arcsec pixels inside the polygon. A pixel is dropped when it is flagged as layover or shadow (bits 4 or 16) in the lsmap of any relative orbit. The outputs are the median co-pol and cross-pol gamma0 in dB, and the median of the per-pixel cross/co ratio in dB. VV+VH is used where the pair exists, otherwise HH+HV. `pol_pair` says which. A site needs at least 30 valid pixels. Otherwise the values are empty (never zero) and `status` and `reason` say why.
- The run reads only HTTP byte ranges and stores nothing on disk. It took 83 to 87 s on the venue network (`data/run_log.csv`). I did not measure the bytes transferred.

## Defects of the specification (issue #20)

- The Accept asks for a summer value for at least 90% of 120 sites. With this login-free data set the most northern and the Putorana sites cannot have one, so the Accept cannot hold. The same defect would hit any single-season Sentinel-1 product in 2020 at Putorana.
- "One late-summer scene with date, orbit direction and incidence angle" assumes scene-level data. The login-free path gives seasonal means instead. The scene-level product (Planetary Computer RTC) needs an account.
- The Arctic sites are HH+HV in part and VV+VH in part. Co-pol HH and VV are not the same quantity, so the comparison below uses VV+VH only and reports HH+HV separately.
