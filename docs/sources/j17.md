**NASADEM mirror and files.** The mirror is OpenTopography's public bucket,
`https://opentopography.s3.sdsc.edu/raster/NASADEM/NASADEM_be/NASADEM_HGT_n35w112.tif` (HTTP 200, 12,669,031
bytes, no login). Header as read:
- GeoTIFF, int16, 3601 x 3601 pixels, 1 arc-second spacing, EPSG:4326.
- `AREA_OR_POINT=Point`, nodata -32768, units m.
- The upper-left pixel centre lies exactly on 36 N, 112 W, so the last row and column are shared with the
  neighbouring tiles.

The Copernicus tile N35W112 has the same transform origin, with 3600 x 3600 float32 pixels, `AREA_OR_POINT=Point`
and no nodata value.

OpenTopography's own metadata page for the dataset
(https://portal.opentopography.org/datasetMetadata?otCollectionID=OT.032021.4326.2, DOI 10.5069/G93T9FD9) says:
- title: "NASADEM Global Digital Elevation Model"; citation: "NASA JPL (2021). NASADEM Merged DEM Global 1 arc
  second V001. Distributed by OpenTopography." The second citation, "NASA JPL. NASADEM Merged DEM Global 1 arc
  second V001. 2020, distributed by NASA EOSDIS Land Processes DAAC", is the NASADEM_HGT product;
- voids filled with other sources, including ASTER GDEM;
- vertical datum EGM96 geoid (EPSG 5773). Copernicus uses EGM2008: the offset between them is smooth and does not
  change slopes;
- licence: "Not Provided".

What "_be" in the folder name means is UNVERIFIED: the page does not mention it. The files carry the NASADEM_HGT
name. NASADEM is radar-derived, so it is a surface model like Copernicus and not true bare earth.

**Grid alignment.** Pixel offsets of 0 / ±1 pixel against the Copernicus tile give median absolute height
differences of 1.38 m at (0,0) and 1.55 to 1.83 m at the other eight offsets. The two tiles are therefore aligned
pixel for pixel. The median height difference NASADEM minus Copernicus is -0.12 m.

How it is implemented:
1. Drop the shared last row and column, leaving 3600 x 3600 pixels.
2. Resample along x with the same bilinear `scipy.ndimage.zoom` to the width that the Copernicus tile of the same
   name gets after its square-pixel resampling. `copdem_columns` gives Copernicus's native columns: 3600 below 50
   degrees, 2400 between 50 and 60 degrees.
3. Return the Copernicus bounds and pixel size.

`copdem_grid(tile)` reproduced the shape, bounds and pixel size of all 38 cached Copernicus tiles exactly. On
N35W112 the cell latitudes and longitudes were identical in all 2,520 cells.

A side finding that affects both DEMs: scipy's zoom sets the last output column to 0. That column lies in the
partial strip that `cell_grid` never uses, unless the width is a multiple of 64.

**MODIS class decoding.** The layer `MODIS_Combined_L3_IGBP_Land_Cover_Type_Annual` is MCD12Q1 v061 (layer
metadata https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov/layer-metadata/v1.0/MODIS_Combined_L3_IGBP_Land_Cover_Type_Annual.json).
WMTS GetCapabilities (https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov/wmts/epsg4326/best/1.0.0/WMTSCapabilities.xml) gives:
- times `2001-01-01/2024-01-01/P1Y`. WMS requests for 2000 and 2025 returned fully transparent images;
- colour map https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov/colormaps/v1.3/MODIS_IGBP_Land_Cover_Type.xml, which is
  one-to-one: 17 classes plus 255.

The WMS PNG comes back as RGBA. Decoding is by exact RGB match with that colour map, which is copied into
`IGBP_RGB`. All 9 colours of the N35W112 image were found in the map.

Groups:

| Group | IGBP classes |
|---|---|
| bare | 16 |
| ice | 15 |
| water | 17 and 0 (they share one colour), plus transparent no-data |
| built | 13 |
| veg | 1-12 and 14 |
| none | 255 |

Edge cases: 7 Open Shrublands and 10 Grasslands count as vegetation, although WorldCover calls much of that ground
"bare / sparse" or "moss and lichen". MODIS "Barren" is therefore stricter.

## Comparison at scale, recommendation, licences (job J17b, Sat 14 Nov 2026, 23:20)

`python -m pipeline.run validate-dem` (`validation/nasadem_vs_copdem.py`, 25 s) wrote
`data/validation/nasadem_vs_copdem.json` and `docs/figures/nasadem_vs_copdem.png`. Two changes to the script
first: it scored against `pipeline.score.DEFAULT_REGION` (`polar_160km`), the target before J18; it now scores
against `DEFAULT_TARGET` (`base_regions`), reports `polar_160km` under `other_targets`, and gives every result a
second time without the cells that carry the dune-field flag. A format error in an axis label stopped the first
run after the numbers were computed; fixed.

**Data.** 618 tiles between 56 S and 60 N; 1,404,145 valid NASADEM cells; 1,118,814 Copernicus cells in those
tiles (the Copernicus scan kept only cells with WorldCover bare of at least 0.5); 1,117,272 cells valid in both.

**Features on the common cells (NASADEM against Copernicus).**

| Feature | Pearson r | Spearman | Median ratio NASADEM / Copernicus | Robust spread of log10 ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| slope at 240 m | 0.999 | 0.996 | 0.993 | 0.020 |
| slope at 570 m | 1.000 | 0.999 | 0.995 | 0.010 |
| roughness at 200 m | 0.959 | 0.942 | 1.030 | 0.175 |
| roughness at 400 m | 0.991 | 0.980 | 0.974 | 0.076 |
| roughness at 800 m | 0.998 | 0.995 | 0.982 | 0.031 |
| roughness at 1,600 m | 0.999 | 0.998 | 0.991 | 0.016 |
| share of closed depressions | 0.758 | 0.469 | 1.75 (cells where both are above 0) | 0.532 |
| depth of closed depressions | 0.815 | 0.647 | 5.54 (cells where both are above 0) | 0.732 |

Slopes and roughness from 400 m up are the same in both models. Roughness at 200 m agrees in the median (ratio
1.03; the 1.56 measured on tile N35W112 was not typical) but scatters. Closed depressions do not agree: the
median share is 0 in Copernicus and 0.0007 in NASADEM, which has small depressions almost everywhere.

**Land-cover mask (bare at least 0.8).** On the cells of the Copernicus tables, MODIS and WorldCover decide
differently for 18.7% (903,345 bare by WorldCover, 760,542 by MODIS, 727,630 by both). On the unfiltered caches
of 74 tiles on this machine: 9.7% of 159,174 cells. MODIS "Barren" is the stricter class.

**Result against the target `base_regions`.**

| | Copernicus + WorldCover | NASADEM + MODIS | NASADEM + WorldCover |
|---|---|---|---|
| candidate cells in the 618 tiles | 33,911 | 2,996 | 4,886 |
| tiles with at least one candidate | 490 | 261 | 316 |
| Spearman of the per-tile candidate share against Copernicus + WorldCover | | **0.637** | 0.694 |
| top-50 tiles shared | | **21 of 50** | 18 of 50 |

2,237 candidate cells are shared by the two full variants: three quarters of the NASA candidates are also ESA
candidates, but only 7% of the ESA candidates are NASA candidates. Per cell, `p_all` has a Pearson correlation of
0.574 and a Spearman correlation of 0.808. Without the flagged cells the tile numbers are 0.700 and 25 of 50;
against `polar_160km` they are 0.623 and 23 of 50. The NASADEM + WorldCover column shows that the elevation model,
not the land-cover map, makes most of the difference. In the figure the tiles with 34 to 68% candidate share
under Copernicus (the sand seas among them) have almost none under NASADEM.

**Recommendation, by the rule of J19** (`pipeline.score.dem_policy_from_rule`: NASADEM primary if the tile
Spearman is at least 0.90 and at least 35 of the top 50 tiles are shared): **not met, 0.637 and 21. Copernicus
stays the elevation model of the ranking; NASADEM is a cross-check.** No threshold was changed.

What the cross-check says, and it has to be said in the README: the slopes do not depend on the elevation
model, the closed-depression features do, and through them the list of candidate cells does. A site that is a
candidate under both models is a stronger claim than a site under one. Which of the two models is closer to the
truth at the scale of small depressions was not tested here (SRTM noise in NASADEM, or smoothing in the
Copernicus product, are both possible).
