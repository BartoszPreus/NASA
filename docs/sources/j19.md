## What was decided, and by which rule

**Elevation model.** `pipeline.score.dem_policy_from_rule()` holds the rule: NASADEM (with MODIS land cover) is
primary for the tiles it covers if `data/validation/nasadem_vs_copdem.json` shows a Spearman correlation of the
per-tile candidate share of at least 0.90 and a top-50 overlap of at least 35, and only if that comparison was
scored against the current target. Result now: **Copernicus**, because the comparison file does not exist (job
J17b is in progress under another agent). `python -m pipeline.sites` prints the policy and the reason in its
first line; `--dem-policy copdem|nasa-first` overrides it; `load_cells(dem_policy=...)` does the work and every
row carries `dem_source`. New stages in `pipeline/run.py`: `scan-nasa` (cloud) and `validate-dem`. Tests:
`tests/test_dem_policy.py` (5 tests: the rule with and without the file, thresholds inclusive, wrong target,
mixing of tiles).

**Target and ranking key.** Kept as J18 set them: target `base_regions`, key `best_window_km2`. The tables of
`docs/sources/j18.md` do not prefer the pooled target: enrichment of known analogs is 0.91 (15 of 35 sites above
the base rate) against 0.97 (16 of 35) for `polar_160km`, and the six reference tiles rank somewhat worse under
it (Atacama core 40 against 18, Mauna Loa 49 against 48, Devon Island 112 against 98 and 538 against 186, Askja
427 against 407, Harrat Khaybar 709 against 442, by summed weighted area). The pooled target stays because the
challenge asks for analogs of the base locations and those regions are steeper than the polar average: a choice
of definition, stated as such in `docs/METHOD.md`. The per-region and polar scores are in every output, so the
app can offer the other targets.

## The run

`python -m pipeline.run sites validate-enrichment context partner-context catalog`: all five stages exit 0
(`data/run_log.csv`: 71 s, 5 s, 278 s, 12 s, 2 s). The ranking is the one of J18, reproduced on a second machine:
the same 200 sites in the same order, identical `best_window_km2`, `weighted_area_km2`, `score` and positions.

NASA context now belongs to this ranking: NASA POWER for the top 150 sites, a NASA Worldview image for the top 60
(before, 2 of the 200 ranked sites had either). The image stage got more candidate dates (2024 and 2023 after
2025): the top-30 images with at least 30% cloud went from 10 to 1.

## Review of the top 30 (`data/sites/top30_review.csv`, contact sheets `docs/figures/top30_sheet_1..3.png`)

Tool: `python -m pipeline.review` (builds the sheets, keeps the verdicts, exits 1 if a top-20 site has no
verdict or is judged an artefact without carrying the flag). The verdicts were written by the conductor (an SI
model) from the images; a person has not looked yet.

- 26 of 30: not an artefact. 4 of 30: unsure, because every candidate image is hazy or under cloud (ranks 14 and
  30 on the northern Ungava Peninsula, ranks 22 and 29 on northern Baffin Island). None: sand sea, lake bed, salt
  flat or glacier. In the top 20: 19 no, 1 unsure (rank 14).
- **The top 30 is a handful of places, not thirty.** 16 sites are pieces of one landform, the Putorana Plateau in
  Siberia (flat-topped basalt tablelands cut by deep valleys, 67 to 70 N); 5 are one dissected, ice-free upland
  in southern Peary Land, north Greenland (82.5 N); 5 are in Arctic Canada (Hazen Plateau on Ellesmere Island,
  northern Baffin Island, northern Ungava); then the Polar Urals, Chukotka, and the two known analogs Atacama
  (rank 8) and Mauna Loa (rank 24). Present regions, not ranks.
- **Vegetation is under-reported in the Arctic.** The land-cover map's class "moss and lichen" counts as bare.
  The plateau tops of Putorana and the hills of Chukotka are tundra; the Putorana valleys are forested. The
  shape is real, the "bare ground" label is generous.
- Ungava (ranks 14 and 30): the Canadian Shield there is covered with small lakes; closed depressions may be
  lake basins. Not to be shown as a candidate until a clear image or a map is checked.
- Two stored images (ranks 1 and 4) are veiled by thin cloud: the stage picks the image with the fewest white
  pixels, and thin grey cloud is not white. The 2025-08-15 images of both showed bare dissected ground.
