# J8 sources: known Moon and Mars analog sites (`data/known_analogs.csv`)

Written with SI assistance (Claude Code). All pages below were opened on 2026-11-14. Coordinates were taken only from these pages; none from memory.

## Summary

- 63 rows, all parse (pandas), lat in [-90, 90], lon in [-180, 180], no duplicate names, no two rows closer than 2 km (closest pair: Cinder Lake Crater Field and Sunset Crater, 6.3 km apart).
- `verified`: 16 `opened` (peer-reviewed paper or agency page), 47 `secondary` (Wikipedia or a GNIS-derived gazetteer).
- By `type`: volcanic 22, impact 14, desert 10, operational 9, polar 6, cave 2.
- By `analog_for`: both 26, moon 19, mars 18.

## Conventions

- DMS values were converted to decimal degrees (4 decimals), west and south negative.
- `radius_km`: 10 for a single crater, cone, lava flow, tube or station; 25 for a volcanic field or a crater over ~20 km; 50 to 150 for regions (Atacama 150, Svalbard 150, Qaidam 150, Dry Valleys 100, Devon Island 100, Makgadikgadi 100, Death Valley 50, Dalangtan area 50). Sudbury (~200 km) and Vredefort (250-300 km) get 100.
- `type` has only six values, so some sites are mapped to the closest one: `desert` also covers arid, evaporite, sedimentary and acid-water sites (Rio Tinto, Laguna de Tirez, Bletterbach, Makgadikgadi, Qaidam); `volcanic` also covers igneous intrusions and mantle rocks (Lofoten and Stillwater anorthosites, Gros Morne Tablelands) and hydrothermal sites (Dallol, Grand Prismatic Spring, Krysuvik); Cinder Lake (man-made explosion craters) is `impact`; Pavilion Lake (underwater-operations analog) is `operational`.
- `analog_for` follows the source's stated target where one is given (Wikipedia table "Moon, Mars" -> `both`; Lambert & Reimold impact sites -> `moon` unless another source also lists Mars; BASALT sites -> `mars`).
- Black Point Lava Flow: the only agency coordinate found is the centre point of ISS astronaut photo ISS020-E-33530 (machine-learning centre 35.69 N, 111.37 W; manual centre 35.7 N, 111.3 W), whose feature list names "BLACK POINT LAVA FLOW". It is a photo centre, not a surveyed point; radius 10 km covers it.
- Cinder Lake Crater Field: the gazetteer point (35.308, -111.505) is ~6 km south of Sunset Crater; the USGS 1967 report text says "2 miles south of Sunset Crater". Treat as approximate (secondary).

## Requested sites not included, and why

- **Mojave Lunar Surface Proving Ground** (Astrobotic): only "Mojave, CA" is published, no coordinates; it is also a commercial facility.
- **Mojave Desert (as a region)**: coordinates exist (Wikipedia, 35 N 116 W) but no opened source calls the Mojave itself a Moon/Mars analog; the NPS page uses "Mars and the Mojave" only as a festival name. Left out; Death Valley and Mars Hill cover the area.
- **Lake Vostok**: coordinates found (Wikipedia, -77.5, 106.0), but sources call it an analog for Europa and Enceladus, not the Moon or Mars, so it does not fit `analog_for`. Concordia is included.
- **Lenghu (Qaidam)**: Wikipedia names only the Lenghu oil field, no coordinates; the Qaidam Basin row (radius 150 km) covers it.
- **Kilauea Iki**: only coordinates found were inside a reference link URL; it lies ~5 km from the Kilauea row (radius 25 km), so it is covered.
- **Pu'u Hiwahine (PISCES 2010 test cone on Mauna Kea)**: no coordinates found; covered by the Mauna Kea row (radius 25 km).
- **Iceland highlands** as a region: not added as a separate row; Askja, Holuhraun, Laki and CHILL-ICE cover it.
- Not attempted for lack of time (CAFE Table 1 names without coordinates in J8): Barberton, Pilbara, Kamchatka, Columbia River basalts, Tuktoyaktuk, Turpan, Morocco (Ibn Battuta), Namibia, Arkaroola, Axel Heiberg springs beyond the station, Eyjafjallajokull.
