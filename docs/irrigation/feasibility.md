# Planning irrigation with a Polish farm’s equipment

**Feasibility study · 21 November 2026 · [Issue 38](https://github.com/BartoszPreus/NASA/issues/38)**

A useful first release answers three questions for tomorrow’s work. **Which field needs water, which source can supply it, and where should the farmer set up the equipment they own?** Poland publishes annual crop polygons and downloadable surface-water features. These can shorten setup, while the farmer supplies the facts that decide whether a run is possible today: what is growing, how much water remains, the pump’s performance under load and which paths a hose or tractor can use. Start with open-field potatoes and a movable sprinkler set on a cooperating farm. Use a daily soil-water balance for timing and a measured sprinkler pattern for placement. Give the farmer a second option when less water requires more labour. The research established data access and checked example calculations. A season of metered field comparisons is still needed to estimate savings.

The team’s earlier *Field Shift* note proposed comparing crop rotations using Earth observations and asked farmers to supply water and equipment constraints. It considered precipitation, evapotranspiration, soil moisture and vegetation indices. The current project also includes a Wielkopolska arable site used as a terrain control. ### The farmer confirms each field and season

On a phone, draw or import a field, select part of a public polygon, split it by crop, mark obstacles and save the management boundary. Ask for crop, sowing or emergence, growth stage, previous rain and irrigation, and usual practice. A suggested crop carries its campaign, source and date retrieved. Preserve corrections and successive crops. Autumn sowing can already belong to the next harvest season. Allow entry from scratch when coverage is incomplete or the field has several overlapping declarations. Calculate actual intersection shares in Polish metric coordinates (EPSG:2180). The cadastral parcel describes property, LPIS describes reference land and GSA describes a declared crop area. The separate public crop-register summaries cannot identify what grows on a particular field. [ARiMR public release](https://www.gov.pl/web/arimr/arimr-otwiera-dane-przestrzenne-ze-swoich-zasobow) · [Register](https://www.gov.pl/web/arimr/rejestr-upraw-pb)

**A reproducible crop check succeeded.** On 21 November, an anonymous WFS request in a broad Wielkopolska window matched **179** arable features for campaign 2026. With its return limit set to three, it yielded three polygons in EPSG:2180. Field `roslina` contained grain maize twice and winter common wheat once. An independent repeat reproduced this result. A subsequent full fetch returned 179 features, of which **166** had a positive-area intersection with the box after geometric clipping. This distinction matters when counting fields or calculating area. It supplies no estimate of agreement with the plants present. The agency describes monthly updates and acknowledges that “część deklaracji nie pokrywa się ze stanem faktycznym.” To repeat the clipping check, increase the linked query’s return limit to 200 and intersect each returned geometry with the projected query rectangle. Store crop year and retrieval time separately from observation and confirmation dates. Show attribution, acquisition time and transformations, and preserve the conditions for sharing original data. The item lists no specific Creative Commons licence. ## Locate water, then check what can actually be delivered

In the Polish farm structure survey, around **46,400 holdings irrigated** agricultural land in good agricultural condition, including covered crops. For the 12 months ending 1 June 2023, **63.8% of irrigating holdings** used groundwater, **20.0%** surface water, **15.7%** a water network, **35.5%** other sources including rainwater and **0.8%** treated wastewater. A holding could use several sources. These percentages count farms and cannot be interpreted as shares of water volume. GUS also reports about 6,900 holdings using sprinklers on 36,800 ha and 19,200 using drip on 39,300 ha. Existing equipment therefore belongs in onboarding, with equipment-specific planning modes. [Statistics Poland, chapter VI, chart 33 and preceding text](https://stat.gov.pl/files/gfx/portalinformacyjny/pl/defaultaktualnosci/5507/5/6/1/charakretystyka_gospodarstw_rolnych_w_2023_r.pdf)

| Where to look | Verified access and what the farmer should do |
|---|---|
| Ponds, reservoirs and surface water | GUGiK distributes BDOT10k by feature class, free for any purpose. In the county 3021 extract, **23** `OT_PTWP_A` polygons classified as standing water intersected the same broad window as the crop query. We intersected actual polygons in EPSG:2180 from row group 29 of the national GeoParquet class file. Use an indexed extract for the field surroundings. The farmer identifies the actual intake, accessible bank and permitted hose route. A mapped bank supplies no live level or pumping capacity. [BDOT10k](https://www.geoportal.gov.pl/en/data/topographic-objects-database-bdot10k/) · [Tested surface-water file](https://opendata.geoportal.gov.pl/bdot10k/schemat2021/GeoParquet/OT_PTWP_A.parquet) |
| Watercourses and standing water | The Wody Polskie MPHP service lists these layers. Its anonymous feature request redirected to a login screen. Validate content type and feature schema before recording an empty result. Use Hydroportal for inspection and the public GUGiK extract for the pilot. [Hydroportal](https://wody.isok.gov.pl/hydroportal.html) · [Service capabilities](https://wody.isok.gov.pl/wss/INSPIRE/INSPIRE_HY_10k_2019_WFS?SERVICE=WFS&REQUEST=GetCapabilities) |
| Wells and groundwater | PIG-PIB Bank HYDRO documents boreholes, including monitoring points. Its regional groundwater maps indicate potential yield. Verify the actual well, access and sustained output during pumping. Detailed record access can require approval. The tested REST preview returned HTTP 403. [Bank HYDRO](https://www.pgi.gov.pl/psh/dane-hydrogeologicznepsh/947-bazy-danych-hydrogeologiczne/9057-bankhydro.html) · [HydroGeoPortal](https://epsh.pgi.gov.pl/start/) |
| Existing private sources | Let the farmer add a GPS pin or tap the map for a well, pond, sealed rain tank, surface intake, authorized supply connection or delivery. Link sources to several fields. Record refill origin, drawdown, usable storage, pump access, current flow, filters, supply windows, costs and verification date. Keep these records private. Leave treated wastewater outside this pilot pending a dedicated water-quality and regulatory design. |

A nearby water body is a candidate. Execution requires confirmed use, physical capacity and an accessible intake. Polish Water Law arts. 33, 34(12), 35 and 389 distinguish ordinary use, special use, water services and device works. Ordinary use can cover a landowner’s own water or groundwater under their land for their own farm, subject to an **annual average at most 5 m³/day**. A mapped stream, parcel boundary or shallow well alone establishes no entitlement. Art. 395 distinguishes works for ordinary-use intakes up to 30 m from abstraction itself. As art. 33(2) says, “Prawo do zwykłego korzystania z wód nie uprawnia do wykonywania urządzeń wodnych bez wymaganej zgody wodnoprawnej.” Store the farmer’s verified basis, rate and cumulative limits, validity period, environmental reserve, supplier conditions and active local restrictions. Never allocate a fresh allowance per field. Refer uncertain cases to the competent authority. [Consolidated Water Law](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/download.xsp/WDU20260001335/T/D20261335L.pdf) · [October amendment](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026/1358/text.pdf) · [Wody Polskie guidance](https://www.gov.pl/web/wody-polskie/pobor-wod-w-ramach-zwyklego-korzystania-a-wymog-posiadania-pozwolenia-wodnoprawnego)

Keep a ledger for each source and shared permission. Reserve planned volumes across fields, reconcile meter readings on completion and release cancelled reservations. For a pond, track opening storage, inflow, withdrawals, seepage, evaporation, spill and the minimum reserve. For a well, check sustainable hourly yield and operating water level. For a network connection, use its authorized delivery pressure, hours and tariff. Count a tank filled from a well once as external abstraction. If supply fails, show how many cubic metres and which field remain short.

## Forecast soil-water demand before choosing a run

| Input | Use and limits |
|---|---|
| Rain already received | Prefer a timed field gauge, then a representative IMGW station with distance and timestamp. Its daily archive listing reached September when checked in November. NASA IMERG Early provides a regional fallback at 0.1°, half-hourly and about four hours behind observations. Store accumulation intervals and convert any rates to depths. Confirm IMGW product terms and the selected IMERG product’s distribution conditions before redistribution. [IMGW feeds](https://danepubliczne.imgw.pl/apiinfo) · [IMGW terms](https://danepubliczne.imgw.pl/pl/introduction) · [IMERG V07](https://gpm.nasa.gov/resources/documents/imerg-v07-technical-documentation) |
| Weather history and outlook | AgERA5 provides daily reference evapotranspiration and rain for historical replay, under a CC-BY licence. Its quality assessment states “Data are delivered with a latency of 8 days.” Rain has about 0.25° effective resolution on its 0.1° grid. Use ECMWF Open Data ensemble forecasts for the next 1–3 days, retaining forecast issue time and each member’s coherent rain, wind and evaporative demand. ECMWF publishes under CC BY 4.0 and its terms. [AgERA5](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/sis-agrometeorological-indicators-timeseries?tab=overview) · [Quality assessment](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/sis-agrometeorological-indicators?tab=quality_assurance_tab) · [ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/open-data) |
| Crop canopy and soil | Compare clear Sentinel-2 L2A red and near-infrared pixels at 10 m over time. Mask clouds/shadows, discard mixed edge pixels and show the last clear date. Investigate anomalous patches with the farmer. SoilGrids 250 m profiles (CC BY 4.0) give an initial soil range. Use downloadable tiles while its beta REST service is paused. Local soil texture, infiltration and probes at different root depths determine whether zones warrant different treatment. Attribute modified Sentinel products as specified. [Sentinel bands](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s2-products) · [Sentinel terms](https://ewds.climate.copernicus.eu/licences/ec-sentinel) · [ISRIC](https://isric.org/explore/soilgrids) · [Soil monitoring](https://extension.umn.edu/natural-resources/conservation/agricultural-soil-and-water/irrigation/soil-moisture-sensors-for-irrigation-scheduling) |

For each crop and soil zone, estimate daily demand using FAO-56 Penman–Monteith reference evapotranspiration (ET₀) and a stage-specific crop coefficient (Kc). With adequate water, ETc = Kc × ET₀. Total available water in millimetres is **TAW = 1,000 × (field capacity − wilting point) × active root depth in metres**, with volumetric soil-water fractions. Readily available water is RAW = p × TAW. Count depletion upward from zero at field capacity. Each day add actual evapotranspiration and deep percolation, subtract infiltrated rain, net irrigation and capillary rise, and keep depletion within available storage. Reduce actual ET when supply limits uptake. Update root depth as plants grow. Initialize from a trusted measurement or carry a wet/dry range. Recent rain alone cannot recover the starting state. [Revised FAO-56, tables 6.1 and 8.1, CC BY 4.0](https://agrhysmo.agr.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FAO56%202025.pdf) · [FAO root-zone accounting, equations 82–88](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e0e.htm)

Replay observations through yesterday. Simulate irrigation and deferral separately under each upcoming weather scenario and compare depletion at the next feasible working window. Show the range and a probability only where the forecast supports one. Recompute after actual rain or irrigation, keeping forecasts out of the observed ledger. A missing gauge reading is unknown. The Polish InHort calculator already illustrates weather, crop and equipment inputs for orchards. IUNG’s drought system supplies regional context, and eDWIN offers agrometeorological services. A useful addition here is the connection between a dated recommendation and a feasible setup using the farm’s water and hardware. [InHort calculator manual](https://www.nawadnianie.inhort.pl/add/instrukcje/Instrukcja%20-%20Potrzeby%20wodne%20roslin.pdf) · [IUNG methodology](https://susza.iung.pulawy.pl/system/) · [eDWIN](https://edwin.gov.pl/)

**Worked example, one hectare of midseason potatoes.** Assume field capacity 0.28, wilting point 0.13, roots 0.50 m, initial depletion 26 mm and ET₀ 4.55 mm/day. FAO’s 2025 potato priors Kc = 1.10 and p ≈ 0.40 at ETc ≈ 5 mm/day give TAW 75 mm and RAW 30 mm. By tomorrow depletion reaches about **31 mm if dry**, or **21 mm** if 10 mm of rain infiltrates in time. In the dry branch, a possible 12 mm net dose leaves about 19 mm depletion. At assumed effective efficiency 80%, that requires 15 mm gross, **150 m³**. At an assumed verified operating point of 30 m³/h and 5 kW it takes five hours and 25 kWh. At illustrative rates of PLN 1/kWh and PLN 0.30/m³, the event costs PLN 70 before labour. A usual 20 mm gross pass would cost PLN 93.33 under the same assumptions. The **50 m³ and PLN 23.33 differences** concern this hypothetical event. One additional hour of labour at PLN 30/h would reverse its cost advantage. Later irrigation, yield and quality determine seasonal value. All farm figures and prices are illustrative. [FAO-56 crop parameters](https://agrhysmo.agr.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FAO56%202025.pdf)

## Choose positions that the pump and operator can serve

**Add equipment** should accept any model, including unlabelled older equipment. Record the pump curve or a measured flow and pressure at the same operating speed, hose lengths and inside diameters, fittings, available heads, nozzle sizes and sector angles. Add available working hours and the time to move a stand or tow a reel. A flow meter and pressure gauge on the normal irrigation circuit give a usable operating point. For long lines, also measure at the farthest head. Record wind, tested throw and a catch-can pattern. Offer a geometry preview while measurements are missing, with a prompt indicating which reading would unlock a schedule. [FAO sprinkler design](https://www.fao.org/4/s8684e/s8684e06.htm) · [EPA hydraulic modelling manual](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P10113EM.TXT)

Polish technical catalogues give concrete acceptance fixtures. An NDJ 233B head with 4.0 and 2.5 mm nozzles at 3 bar delivers 1.49 m³/h with a listed 14.5 m throw. On a regular 15 × 15 m interior grid this averages **6.62 mm/h**, consistent with the catalogue’s 6.6. Edges, terrain and wind can produce quite different local depths. For a travelling reel, the Łukomet IRTEC G231,110G4S/810 table gives **37.4 m³/h, 6.6 bar at the reel inlet and a nominal 62 m strip** for its 24 mm nozzle. A WH20 pump drawing alone from unpressurized water at the same elevation cannot supply this combination. Its maximum flow is 27 m³/h and shutoff pressure 5 bar, at opposite ends of the curve. The interface can explain this mismatch before drawing a pass. These are catalogue checks, with no assertion of stock levels or licence to copy full tables. [Budmech pp. 37–38](https://budmech.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Katalog-Systemy-nawadniania.pdf) · [Łukomet technical table](https://www.lukomet.pl/nawodnienia/g-szpulowe-deszczownie/g200-irtec/struktura-g4-specjal-deszczownie-szpulowe-irtec-typ-g4-specjal) · [Honda catalogue and curves](https://www.mojahonda.pl/download/agregaty/katalog_agregaty_motopompy_Honda.pdf)

For **movable heads**, generate alternative grid orientations, offsets, sectors and combinations of simultaneously operating heads. For **reels**, generate towable lanes and retrieval speeds, accounting for crop rows, headlands, access, hose length and end effects. For existing **drip** systems, schedule row or valve groups using emitter spacing, discharge, filtration and wetted soil fraction. A permanently installed system mainly offers a choice of sector and runtime. Match the method to the farmer’s installed hardware, crop and stage, soil intake rate, foliage sensitivity and labour. Keep a future equipment purchase in a separate investment comparison. [University of Georgia equipment selection](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/B882/factors-to-consider-in-selecting-a-farm-irrigation-system/)

For each candidate, solve the pump operating point against route lift, friction and fittings. Check residual pressure at each head, combined flow, source reserves and shared-pump time conflicts. Apply its field-tested, wind-adjusted distribution to equal-area cells. Add contributions from overlapping heads over the proposed duration. Test the crop target, maximum application intensity and runoff, edge under-application, overspray outside the field, and zero-spray exclusions such as roads. A uniform circle suffices for a reach preview. Actual deposition requires a nozzle pattern or field calibration. For feasible layouts, choose durations that meet the agreed crop target across the selected weather scenarios. Compare **lowest withdrawal** and **lowest operating cost**, including kWh or fuel, water tariff, labour and moves. Without a crop adequacy constraint, switching off every pump wins a water-only objective. If no feasible layout exists, show the binding constraint and the uncovered area. [FAO application rate, overlap and wind](https://www.fao.org/4/s8684e/s8684e06.htm) · [NRCS sprinkler engineering, chapter 11](https://directives.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files2/1720457837/Chapter%2011%20-%20Sprinkler%20Irrigation.pdf)

The Polish-language field card should show field and crop, start window, source, equipment, stand positions or lanes, run order, minutes, gross m³, PLN, remaining source balance and reason for waiting or acting. On return, enter actual meter readings, duration and rainfall. Cache the last downloaded plan for the field. Display its issue time and any changes in wind, source capacity or observed rainfall. Offline edits queue with unique event IDs so a retry or two devices cannot debit the same withdrawal twice.

## Deliver the pilot in independently testable stages

| Stage and proposed owner | Depends on | Deliverable and acceptance check |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Field and inventory · frontend / farm adviser | Cooperating grower | Separate site route with field drawing/import, seasonal crop confirmation, private intake pins, pump, hoses and available heads. Farmer can add unlisted equipment, share one source across two fields and keep a crop correction after a data refresh. |
| 2. Public data · geospatial engineer | Field geography | A server-side service clips versioned ARiMR, BDOT and NMT data in metres. Cache official sheets and record source, licence and age. Test missing crop, service login HTML, nodata, holes and a field straddling two crop polygons. Public tiles contain no farm equipment or private intakes. |
| 3. Daily recommendation · agronomist / data engineer | 1, weather feeds and local calibration | Versioned FAO crop tables, observed event ledger and dated forecast scenarios. Reproduce the potato example, conserve mass through heavy rain, distinguish a missing day from a measured zero and prevent future rain entering yesterday’s record. |
| 4. Movable sprinkler planner · irrigation engineer | 1–3, equipment and intake tests | Compare current and alternative schedules on the same soil target. Reproduce the catalogue checks, reject insufficient pressure or a double-booked pump, protect exclusions, and give the remaining shortfall when a source runs out. Introduce reels after lane, speed and edge tests on a real machine. |
| 5. Field comparison · grower / evaluation lead | Calibrated 1–4 | Pre-register crop/yield tolerance with the farmer. Retain existing practice as control and record water, power or fuel, working time, coverage and marketable yield. Expand to new equipment or crops when the field measurements support transfer. |

Polish potato experiments illustrate why the choice of control matters. Near Bydgoszcz, mean marketable yields on very light soil in dry years were 29.9 t/ha with 180 mm irrigation and 7.9 t/ha rainfed. Wet-year figures were 28.9 t/ha with 70 mm and 21.7 t/ha rainfed. This measures the value of irrigation under those conditions. In Veneto maize, a sensor-informed treatment received 94 mm against 120 mm under farmer dosing, with yields of 18.25 and 18.50 t/ha. Each used different hardware in a single field-season. Neither study isolates the value of this proposed planner. [Rolbiecki et al., table 2](https://www.iitf.lbtu.lv/conference/proceedings2019/Papers/N431.pdf) · [Veneto field study, tables 1 and 4](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/water/articles/10.3389/frwa.2021.627551/full)

Pair comparable field blocks by crop, stage and soil, randomize within pairs and keep equipment consistent. Where replication allows, cross farmer/model timing with existing/proposed layout to distinguish the two effects. Measure local rain, independent root-zone moisture, pressure at the distal head, applied depth in catch cans including edges, and withdrawals at the meter. Within each uniform target zone, calculate lower-quarter distribution uniformity as mean depth in the driest quarter divided by mean depth. Report m³/ha, kWh or litres of fuel/ha, PLN/ha with labour, crop stress, marketable yield and quality. Compare seasonal gross margins and paired uncertainty at block level. Replay only forecasts actually available on each decision date. Hold out farms or seasons and compare adding terrain and imagery to a weather-and-soil baseline. The study did not run continuous weather ingestion, acquire a new Sentinel scene, measure a farm’s pump or conduct a grower trial. A first farm can establish usability, pressure feasibility and measurement quality. A transferable savings claim needs replicated observations with acceptable crop outcomes.
